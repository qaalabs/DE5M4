# DE5M4 - The Data Engineering Product Lifecycle

## Day 1 - Agile Foundations

Day 1 sets the context for the whole module: before you build anything, you need to understand how to plan and organise the work. The Agile framework you explore today will underpin the planning project at the end of Day 4.

### Morning - Introductions and Design Thinking

You'll start by meeting the group and getting set up. The day opens with a discussion - "I want to build a wall" - which sounds simple but quickly surfaces how much can go wrong when requirements are not defined clearly. It is a quick, practical way into the problem the rest of the day addresses.

From there, you will spend time in groups investigating how products actually get designed. You will look at the steps involved in taking an idea through to something buildable, then apply that process to a realistic scenario and share your approach with the room.

### Afternoon - Agile Artefacts and Sprint Planning

The afternoon moves from design thinking into the specific artefacts Agile teams use to organise their work. You will be introduced to the Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, and Increment, as well as Epics and User Stories as a way of describing work at different levels of detail.

You will then put this into practice: first by identifying Epics from a product scenario, then writing User Stories in groups and sharing them back. The day closes with Planning Poker - a technique for estimating effort - and a group sprint planning exercise where you will map out a two-week sprint.

---

## Day 2 - Technical Foundation

Day 2 covers two technical foundations that sit underneath almost everything in data engineering: version control with Git and GitHub, and cloud infrastructure. Both will appear again in later modules.

### Morning - Git and GitHub

The morning is hands-on from the start. You will work through a series of short labs - working with Git locally, creating a repository on GitHub, raising an issue, forking a repository, and opening a pull request. By the end of the morning you will have touched all the core Git workflows a data engineering team uses day to day.

### Afternoon - Cloud Fundamentals

The afternoon shifts to cloud infrastructure. You will start by investigating the core building blocks of cloud platforms - compute, storage, networking - in groups, then share your findings. The second half looks at how those building blocks combine into real architectures: you will work through some cloud architecture scenarios and finish with a discussion about the architecture your own workplace uses.

---

## Day 3 - Data Modeling

Day 3 is split between data modeling and cloud storage. The modeling work builds the analytical thinking you will need when designing data products; the storage labs give you hands-on time with the platforms where data actually lives.

### Morning

The morning includes a demonstration of Docker containers - a technology you will encounter in later modules when working with reproducible environments.

### Afternoon - Data Modeling and Cloud Storage

The afternoon opens with a structured data modeling exercise. Starting from a set of requirements, you will work through conceptual modeling (building an entity-relationship diagram) and then move to a logical model, making and documenting design decisions along the way. Groups present their diagrams to the room.

After the break, you will move into labs covering the three main cloud storage platforms: Amazon S3, Azure Blob Storage, and Google Cloud Storage. Each lab is self-contained, so you will get a feel for how each platform works in practice.

---

## Day 4 - Integration Project

Day 4 brings the module together. The morning is analytical, and the afternoon is an individual planning project that draws on everything from the previous three days.

### Morning - Cloud Pricing

The morning is spent on cloud pricing calculators. You will start with straightforward calculations and move to more complex scenarios, working through realistic decisions about which services to use and what they cost.

### Afternoon - Data Product Planning

The afternoon is the capstone of the module. You will be given a brief and work independently to plan a data engineering product - thinking through requirements, design, and delivery in the way you have practised across the week. At the end of the day, each learner presents their plan to the group. The day closes with a short evaluation.
