# Trainer Prompts (Planning & Sustainability)

Use these to steer discussion **without giving answers**.

## Session 1

### Collating and refining requirements

**Your role in this session**

- Resist the urge to clarify the scenario
- Let learners surface ambiguity
- Push them to prioritise

### Core prompts

Use these selectively:

- "Who is asking for this, and why?"
- "Which requirements are explicit, and which are implied?"
- "What feels like noise rather than need?"
- "If you had to drop one requirement, which would it be?"

### Sustainability nudge (light)

- "What work is currently being duplicated?"
- "Where is effort being wasted today?"

**What good looks like**

- Fewer, clearer requirements
- Evidence of evaluation and refinement
- Learners comfortable saying "this is not needed"

---

## Session 2

### Design options and trade-offs

**Your role**

- Force choice
- Prevent "we could do both"

### Core prompts

- "What options did you consider?"
- "Which option did you reject?"
- "What problem does your chosen option solve best?"

### Cost and sustainability nudge

- "Which option would cost more over time?"
- "What scales as data volume grows?"
- "Where would compute or storage increase unnecessarily?"

**What good looks like**

- Clear decisions
- Explicit rejection of alternatives
- Reasoning, not preference

---

## Session 3

### Lifecycle and long-term thinking

**Your role**

- Shift them from build-only thinking
- Introduce time as a factor

### Core prompts

- "What happens after this goes live?"
- "What runs daily? What runs constantly?"
- "What would break first if usage doubled?"

### Sustainability nudge

- "Where does inefficiency grow quietly?"
- "What becomes expensive or hard to maintain over time?"

**What good looks like**

- Awareness of operational impact
- Recognition that maintenance is part of design
- Simplicity valued over cleverness

---

## Session 4

### Explaining decisions clearly

**Your role**

- Coach articulation, not correctness
- Encourage explicit language

### Core prompts

- "What was the requirement?"
- "What options did you consider?"
- "What did you choose and why?"

### Sustainability language prompt

If they miss it, gently add:

- "How is that more sustainable?"
- "What does this reduce or avoid?"

Encourage phrases like:

- reduced compute
- less duplication
- easier to maintain
- long-term efficiency

**What good looks like**

- Clear, structured explanations
- Sustainability named explicitly
- Confidence in justification

---

## Wrap-up

**Final reflection prompts**

- "Where have you already done this in your job?"
- "What decision could you justify more clearly next time?"

No writing. No sharing required.

---

## Why these prompts matter

They:

- keep the day consistent across cohorts
- protect you from over-teaching
- make the EPA links emerge naturally
- reduce cognitive load for learners

This is the point where the day becomes **repeatable**.
