# Agile Concepts and Artefacts

*Sprints* provide the delivery cycle, while the *Product Backlog*, *Sprint Backlog*, and *Increment* are the **core Agile artefacts**. *Epics* and *User Stories* are **backlog artefacts** that help us organise and explain work.

---

## Epics

An **epic** is a large piece of work that is too big to complete in a single sprint. It groups related user stories around a shared goal or capability. Epics help teams organise and plan work over a longer period without going into fine detail too early. As understanding improves, epics are broken down into smaller stories, making the epic an artefact that captures evolving scope rather than fixed requirements.


## User Stories

A **user story** is a short description of a requirement written from the perspective of the user or stakeholder. It focuses on value and intent rather than technical implementation. User stories act as placeholders for conversation, encouraging discussion between the team and stakeholders before and during development. When supported by acceptance criteria, they become a clear artefact for planning, delivery, and assessment.

---

## Product Backlog

The **Product Backlog** is an ordered list of everything that might be needed in the product. It represents *what* the team could work on and *why* it matters. Items are written in a way that helps the team understand value, priority, and intent, not detailed solutions. As the product and understanding evolve, the Product Backlog changes too – it is a living artefact that shows current thinking and direction.

## Sprint

**A sprint** is a fixed-length period of time, usually 1 to 4 weeks, during which a team works to deliver a usable piece of the product (called an increment). It provides a clear structure for planning, building, and reviewing work. At the start of the sprint, the team selects a small set of high-value items from the Product Backlog and commits to delivering them. By the end of the sprint, those items should be completed to a usable standard.

Sprints create a regular rhythm of delivery and feedback. Rather than trying to plan everything upfront, teams work in short cycles, learn from what they build, and adapt their plans as they go.

## Sprint Backlog

The **Sprint Backlog** is the team's plan for the current sprint. It contains the Product Backlog items selected for that sprint, along with a clear idea of how the team intends to deliver them. This artefact makes work visible day to day and supports collaboration and adaptation. If plans change during the sprint, the Sprint Backlog is updated to reflect that learning.

## Increment

The **Increment** is the sum of all completed work at the end of a sprint, integrated with everything built before. It must be in a usable state and meet the team’s agreed quality standards. The Increment is the most concrete Agile artefact because it provides evidence of real progress. In data engineering, this might be a working pipeline, validated dataset, or deployed configuration.
