    Day 4 (Module 4) – Planning & Sustainability Session

    EPA alignment (at a high level)

    The day supports:

    • K7 – sustainable data products and ESG principles
    • K12 – costing and building systems with net-zero considerations
    • K9 – compliance-aware design (through constraints in scenarios)
    • K13 – financial, security, scalability, and deployment trade-offs

    This is achieved through discussion and justification, not written evidence.

    Rationale and delivery approach

    Purpose of the day

    This day is designed to develop planning, judgement, and decision-making skills, rather than technical build skills.

    Apprentices are not expected to write EPA evidence or produce a finished design. Instead, the focus is on practising the thinking and justification that underpin EPA requirements around sustainability, cost, and governance.

    Why this is structured as a scenario-based day

    The EPA criteria assess whether apprentices can:

    • collate, evaluate, and refine business requirements
    • make defensible design decisions
    • consider sustainability, cost, and long-term impact

    These skills are best developed through:

    • realistic but incomplete scenarios
    • discussion and trade-offs
    • forced decisions rather than open-ended exploration

    A single scenario used across the day mirrors how planning works in real roles and avoids fragmented activities.

    Why there is no building, tooling, or report writing

    This day intentionally avoids:

    • coding or configuration
    • cloud pricing tools
    • EPA report drafting

    Those skills are addressed elsewhere in the module.

    The aim here is to:

    • slow learners down
    • make decision-making visible
    • help them articulate why choices are made

    This supports later EPA work without crossing into coaching.

    How the four sessions work together

    Each session uses the same scenario, but asks a different question:

    1. Session 1 – Requirements Learners practise extracting, evaluating, and refining business needs from messy inputs.

    2. Session 2 – Design decisions Learners must choose between options and explicitly reject alternatives, considering cost and sustainability.

    3. Session 3 – Lifecycle thinking Learners examine how systems behave over time and where inefficiency and waste can grow.

    4. Session 4 – Explanation Learners practise explaining one decision clearly and confidently, including sustainability impacts.

    This progression mirrors real planning work and naturally supports EPA criteria.

    Why groups have different scenarios

    Each group receives a slightly different organisational context, while keeping the same structure and learning goals.

    This:

    • prevents “same as group 1” reporting
    • encourages richer discussion
    • allows the facilitator to probe different angles (scale, compliance, security, cost)

    The activities and prompts remain consistent across groups.

    Why this approach works

    • Reduces cognitive load for learners
    • Encourages deeper thinking rather than surface answers
    • Works for mixed-ability groups
    • Is repeatable and consistent across cohorts

    Most importantly, it builds confidence in explaining decisions, which is a key gap identified in EPA submissions.
