# A Gentler Introduction to Agile for Data Engineers

## Data Engineering Pain Points Approach

This approach uses shared experiences as the entry point to Agile, making it relatable and non-threatening.

### Activity Overview (20-25 minutes)

**Format:** Facilitated discussion with small group sharing

#### Step 1: Individual Reflection (5 minutes)

Ask participants to reflect individually on their experiences with data projects:

Take a few minutes to think about a challenging data project you've worked on. What were the biggest pain points or frustrations? Write down 2-3 specific examples.

Prompt with examples if needed:

- Requirements that kept changing
- Stakeholders not available for questions
- Discovering data quality issues late in the project
- Project scope that kept expanding
- Technical debt that slowed progress
- Difficulty estimating how long tasks would take

#### Step 2: Small Group Sharing (7-8 minutes)

In breakout rooms of 3-4 people:

Share your top pain points with your group. Listen for common themes and patterns. As a group, identify the 3 most significant challenges you've all experienced.

#### Step 3: Gallery of Challenges (5 minutes)

Return to main room and have each group briefly share their top 3 challenges.

Create a virtual "gallery" of these challenges on a shared whiteboard or document.

#### Step 4: Introducing Agile as the Response (7-8 minutes)

Present how Agile principles directly address these common challenges:

What we've just created is essentially a list of the exact problems that led to the creation of Agile methodologies. Let's see how Agile principles address these specific challenges.

Map common challenges to Agile solutions:

| Common Challenge | Agile Response |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Changing requirements | Embracing change through iterative development |
| Stakeholder availability | Regular collaboration built into the process |
| Late discovery of issues | Early and continuous delivery to find problems sooner |
| Scope expansion | Time-boxed iterations and prioritization |
| Technical debt | Sustainable pace and technical excellence |
| Difficult estimation | Relative sizing and learning from past performance |

### Why This Approach Works:

1. **Starts with Empathy**: Acknowledges real challenges participants have faced
2. **Creates Relevance**: Positions Agile as a solution to their actual problems
3. **Non-threatening**: Doesn't require prior knowledge or complex activities
4. **Builds Community**: Shows participants they share common struggles
5. **Problem-First Approach**: Establishes the "why" before the "what" of Agile

## Simple Follow-Up Visual Activity (Optional, 10-15 minutes)

If you want a very light hands-on component, add this simple visualization:

### "From Waterfall to Agile" Activity

Provide a simple diagram showing a traditional waterfall process for a data project:

1. Requirements (2 months)
2. Design (1 month)
3. Development (3 months)
4. Testing (1 month)
5. Deployment (2 weeks)

Ask small groups (same breakouts) to:

1. Identify where problems typically occur in this approach for data projects
2. Sketch how they would restructure this into shorter iterations
3. Indicate where they would add feedback loops and stakeholder checkpoints

Have groups share their redesigned approaches, then show a simple Agile alternative with 2-week sprints that include all phases in miniature.

## Transitioning to Frameworks

After this gentle introduction through shared experiences, you'll have established a solid foundation to introduce specific frameworks:

Now that we understand the problems Agile aims to solve, let's look at some specific frameworks that implement these principles. These are different approaches to achieving the same goals we just discussed."

You can then briefly introduce Scrum, Kanban, and other frameworks as different "flavors" of implementing the same core principles, each with particular strengths for different contexts.

This approach creates a natural, conversational entry into Agile concepts that builds on participants' existing knowledge and experiences, making it more accessible and immediately relevant to their data engineering work.
