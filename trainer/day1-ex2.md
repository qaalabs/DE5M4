# Introducing Agile: An Effective Introductory Exercise

## "The Evolving Data Dashboard" Exercise

This exercise simulates building a data product with changing requirements, demonstrating core Agile values while being directly relevant to data engineering.

### Exercise Overview (30-35 minutes total)

**Premise:**
Participants work in small teams to design a data dashboard for a business stakeholder. However, requirements will change during the exercise, simulating real-world conditions and highlighting the need for Agile approaches.

### Setup and Materials:
- Virtual whiteboard space for each team
- Pre-prepared "requirement change cards" to introduce during the activity
- Simple dashboard template with placeholder sections
- Timer for iteration timeboxes

### Instructions:

#### Introduction (3 minutes)
We're going to experience firsthand why Agile methodologies have become essential in today's data engineering projects. Each team will work on designing a business dashboard, but you'll need to adapt to changing requirements along the way - just like in real projects.

#### Step 1: Initial Requirements (2 minutes)
Present the initial dashboard request:

"The Marketing Director needs a dashboard showing daily website traffic by source, conversion rates, and top-performing content. The dashboard needs to be updated daily and should help optimize marketing spend."

#### Step 2: First Design Iteration (7 minutes)
Teams work on their first dashboard design using the template provided. They should:
- Sketch what metrics will be displayed
- Note what data sources would be needed
- Indicate how often data would refresh
- Show how information would be visualized

#### Step 3: First Requirement Change (1 minute)
Announce the first change:

"The CEO has just joined the project. She wants the dashboard to also show how website metrics correlate with actual sales data. This is now a high priority."

#### Step 4: Second Design Iteration (5 minutes)
Teams revise their designs to incorporate the new requirement.

#### Step 5: Second Requirement Change (1 minute)
Announce another change:

"The IT Security team has flagged that customer-level data cannot be included in the dashboard due to new privacy regulations. You need to adjust your design to comply."

#### Step 6: Third Design Iteration (5 minutes)
Teams revise again to address security concerns.

#### Step 7: Final Requirement Change (1 minute)
One final change:

"Good news! The dashboard has been prioritized for release next week instead of next month. You need to identify a minimum viable version that can be delivered quickly."

#### Step 8: Final Design Iteration (5 minutes)
Teams create their MVP design, focusing on what's most important.

#### Debrief Discussion (10 minutes)
Guide a reflection on the experience:

- "How did your designs evolve across the iterations?"
- "What was challenging about adapting to the changing requirements?"
- "What would have made this process easier?"
- "How does this relate to real data engineering projects you've worked on?"

Then introduce key Agile principles that address these challenges:
- Responding to change over following a plan
- Working solutions over comprehensive documentation
- Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
- Individuals and interactions over processes and tools

### Why This Exercise Works:

1. **Experiential Learning**: Participants feel the challenges of changing requirements firsthand
2. **Relevant Context**: Uses a data-specific scenario that engineers can relate to
3. **Demonstrates Need**: Creates a clear "why Agile matters" narrative before diving into methodologies
4. **Accessible Entry Point**: Requires no prior Agile knowledge to participate
5. **Sets Up Framework Comparison**: Creates a perfect segue to discuss how different Agile frameworks (Scrum, Kanban, XP) would handle these challenges

## Following Up: Framework Introduction

After this exercise, you'll have a natural opening to introduce specific Agile frameworks by connecting them to the challenges experienced:

"Now that we've experienced the challenges of building data products with evolving requirements, let's explore different Agile frameworks that help teams navigate these situations effectively."

Then you can briefly introduce:

1. **Scrum**: For structured iterations with defined roles and ceremonies
2. **Kanban**: For visualizing workflow and managing capacity
3. **Extreme Programming**: For technical excellence practices like TDD and pair programming

For each framework, highlight specifically how it would address the challenges they just experienced in the exercise.

This approach creates a much stronger foundation than beginning with a theoretical comparison to waterfall or a lecture on Agile history. By starting with an experience that demonstrates the need, participants will be more receptive to learning about specific methodologies and practices.
