### Scenario A - Retail Company

A retail company with 200 physical stores and an e-commerce website wants to create a data product that <mark>helps them understand customer behavior</mark> across both channels to improve inventory management and personalization. They have point-of-sale data, online transaction records, customer profiles, and inventory data available.

### Scenario B - Healthcare Provider

A regional healthcare network with 5 hospitals and 20 clinics wants to create a data product that <mark>predicts patient readmission</mark> risk within 30 days of discharge. They have electronic health records, medication data, demographic information, and historical admission records available, but data is currently siloed across different systems.

### Scenario C - Manufacturing Company

A manufacturing company operating 3 factories wants to create a data product that <mark>optimises production scheduling and reduces equipment downtime</mark>. They have sensor data from machinery, production logs, quality control measurements, maintenance records, and supply chain data, but currently rely on manual reporting and Excel spreadsheets.

### Scenario D - Financial Services

A mid-sized bank wants to create a data product that <mark>detects potentially fraudulent transactions in real-time</mark> while minimising false positives that frustrate customers. They have transaction histories, customer profiles, merchant data, and historical fraud cases, but need to balance security with customer experience.
